About

Teaching, research and open knowledge

I'm a professional teacher with the Ghana Education Service, and I'm completing an MPhil in Cybersecurity and Digital Forensics. Most of my work sits where technology, language and community meet.

As a Wikimedia organizer I have spent years making the Dagbani language and other Gur/Mabia languages of West Africa visible and searchable online. A language with no digital presence is easy to lose, and I'd like to help change that for the ones I work with.

My research uses hybrid multi-modal deep learning to detect deepfake video. I'm also active in student leadership, digital literacy and gender equity, and I've been involved in leadership since 2006.

Interests

  • Cybersecurity and AI. Deepfake detection, digital forensics.
  • Language digitization. NLP, corpus building and voice data for African languages.
  • Open knowledge. Wikipedia, Wikidata and free access to information.
  • Gender and equity. Digital inclusion and leadership.
  • Biodiversity. Documenting local ecological knowledge in Dagbani.
  • Internet governance. Policy and digital rights.

Research

Detecting deepfake video

MPhil dissertation · In progress

A Hybrid Multi-Modal Deep Learning Framework for the Detection of Deepfake Videos

The dissertation builds a framework that combines facial dynamics, audio-visual synchrony, biological signals and spatiotemporal artifacts, with the aim of detecting synthetic media more reliably than single-modal methods.

It has already produced a PRISMA-compliant systematic literature review, "Single-Modal versus Hybrid Multi-Modal Frameworks for the Detection of Deepfake Videos", being prepared for IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security.
