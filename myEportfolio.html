I am a professional educator with the Ghana Education Service and an MPhil candidate in Cybersecurity & Digital Forensics. My work lives at the intersection of technology, language, and community — building bridges between African indigenous knowledge and the global digital commons.

As an active Wikimedia organizer, I have spent years making the Dagbani language and other Gur/Mabia languages of West Africa visible, searchable, and alive on the internet. A language without a digital footprint is a language on the edge of erasure — and I am determined to change that.

My research focuses on AI-powered deepfake video detection, developing hybrid multi-modal deep learning frameworks to combat synthetic media and protect the integrity of digital information. I am also a committed student leader, digital literacy advocate, and passionate believer in gender equity and transformational leadership.

Since 2006 — from student union halls to international Wikimedia conferences — I have never stopped building the communities and tools that make knowledge more open, more equitable, and more human.

Research & Publications

MPhil Dissertation · Ongoing

A Hybrid Multi-Modal Deep Learning Framework for the Detection of Deepfake Videos

Deepfake technology poses an existential threat to digital truth. My dissertation develops a novel hybrid framework combining facial dynamics, audio-visual synchrony, biological signals, and spatiotemporal artifacts — detecting AI-generated synthetic media with greater precision than single-modal approaches.

This work produced a PRISMA-compliant systematic literature review: "Single-Modal versus Hybrid Multi-Modal Frameworks for the Detection of Deepfake Videos" — a publication-ready manuscript targeting IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security.
